# Solar Splits Between Protected Utility-Scale Backlogs and a Residential Policy Reset

**Why this is trending:** Solar stocks enter 2026 with diverging fortunes by segment: utility-scale demand is accelerating on AI data-center power needs and Goldman Sachs Buy ratings, while residential solar faces a real reset after the federal 25D tax credit for residential solar loans and cash sales expired at the end of 2025, with further legislative uncertainty over commercial tax-credit transferability still being debated.

**Linked companies:** First Solar (FSLR), Enphase Energy (ENPH)

## Bull case

Goldman Sachs named First Solar a standout for its booking outlook, potential for higher average selling prices, and capacity-expansion scope, expecting utility-scale solar deployment to grow roughly 3% in 2026 with sector revenue growth closer to 15% as companies gain pricing power. First Solar's backlog is contracted through 2029, and its utility-scale investment tax credit and domestic-content bonus are preserved under the current legislative language, insulating it from residential-side policy risk. Enphase Energy carries the highest gross margin in its peer group (around 54%) and beat Q4 2025 earnings estimates, with its new commercial microinverter launch diversifying the company toward the less policy-exposed commercial rooftop segment.

## Bear case

Goldman itself expects "another reset" in the US residential solar market following the 25D tax-credit expiration, which should weigh on near-term residential demand even as medium-term support remains from rising utility rates. Ongoing legislative debate over further reductions to residential tax credits and restrictions on commercial tax-credit transfers adds continued policy uncertainty across the sector. Enphase's business, despite its margin strength, remains meaningfully tied to residential rooftop demand and net-metering policy, which has already been reduced by roughly 75% in a state as large as California — exposing it more directly to the residential reset than a pure utility-scale name like First Solar.

## Risks

- **Policy risk:** further legislative changes to residential and commercial solar tax credits remain under active debate and could reduce demand or margins depending on final terms.
- **Segment-divergence risk:** this basket intentionally spans a policy-protected utility-scale name and a more residential-exposed name; a worse-than-expected residential reset could weigh on Enphase specifically even if First Solar's protected backlog performs as expected.
- **Rate-sensitivity risk:** solar project economics are sensitive to financing costs; higher-for-longer interest rates could pressure both segments' project economics independent of the tax-credit questions above.
- **Concentration risk:** a two-name basket means company-specific execution (a delayed facility, a missed earnings quarter) affects the whole basket disproportionately.
