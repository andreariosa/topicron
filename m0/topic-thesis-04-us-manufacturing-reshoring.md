# US Manufacturing Reshoring Reaches Decade-High Growth Forecasts

**Why this is trending:** Coverage through early 2026 describes over $3 trillion in reshoring-related investment announcements since early 2025, driven by tariff policy and supply-chain security concerns. Morgan Stanley's research team forecasts the US industrial economy will reach decade-high growth rates in the second half of 2026 into 2027, framing it as a secular shift rather than a typical cyclical upturn.

**Linked companies:** GE Aerospace (GE), Caterpillar (CAT), EnerSys (ENS)

## Bull case

The reshoring wave may be a secular, durable opportunity rather than a normal demand cycle: it's framed as being fueled by a roughly $1.2 trillion trade deficit and a narrowing gap between domestic production and consumption, with rising industrial machinery orders acting as an "on-ramp" for a coming wave of new factory construction. Caterpillar's concrete action — shifting construction-equipment production from Japan to Georgia and Texas, backed by a $725 million capacity expansion — illustrates the kind of company-level commitment behind the broader trend, and 2026 earnings estimates across reshoring-focused manufacturers have been revised upward.

## Bear case

The tariff policy driving this reshoring wave is itself a source of legal and political uncertainty — one report describes a Supreme Court ruling that briefly challenged the underlying trade regime before being met with an executive response that kept the policy pressure in place, meaning the durability of the tariff structure isn't fully settled. Separately, domestic manufacturing costs remain structurally higher than many offshore alternatives, so realized margins may depend on automation and workforce-productivity gains that aren't guaranteed at scale; some coverage also points to late-2025 weakness in industrial orders tied to cost pressures, suggesting the "decade-high growth" forecast isn't yet confirmed by realized order data.

## Risks

- **Legal/policy risk:** the tariff regime underlying the reshoring incentive has already faced at least one legal challenge; a change in tariff policy could remove the economic rationale for continued reshoring investment.
- **Cost/margin risk:** domestic manufacturing costs remain structurally higher than many offshore alternatives; the thesis depends on productivity and automation gains offsetting that gap, which isn't guaranteed at scale.
- **Cyclicality risk:** some coverage describes late-2025 weakness in industrial orders tied to cost pressures, suggesting the growth forecast isn't yet confirmed by realized data.
- **Concentration risk:** a three-name basket means company-specific execution on reshoring projects (delays, cost overruns) affects the whole basket disproportionately.
