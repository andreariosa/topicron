# US wireless carriers after the SpaceX spectrum deal (one-page version)

*A short restatement of `m0/topic-thesis-13-us-wireless-spacex.md`, written for readers who want the argument in about three minutes. If the two disagree, the full text wins. It makes no claim about performance: the tracking window starts at publication and results are reported at 30, 60 and 90 days.*

**Disclosure.** Written with AI assistance (Claude); the figures were checked against company releases, FCC orders and Treasury data (links at the end). This is a hypothesis being tracked over 90 days, not investment advice. Prices are as of the 9 October 2026 close.

**Hypothesis.** Over the next 90 days a basket of Verizon (40%), AT&T (40%) and T-Mobile (20%) beats the S&P 500 by at least 5 percentage points on price return, as the 9 October selloff partly reverses.

**Why now.** On 8 Oct SpaceX agreed to buy Grain Management's nationwide 800 MHz licences (up to 14 MHz paired; price undisclosed, the WSJ reported $8bn in cash; FCC approval needed). On 9 Oct T-Mobile fell 13.3%, AT&T 10.8% and Verizon 10.1%, while the S&P rose 0.6%. About 1.1 and 1.5 points of the AT&T and Verizon moves were the dividend going ex that day.

**What the market may be missing.** A 2027 event priced into 2026 stocks:

- SpaceX itself expects Starlink Mobile service around the end of 2027, and the FCC ties its authority over the EchoStar spectrum to a closing expected around 30 Nov 2027.
- The Grain licences carry FCC dates: partner selection by 5 Nov 2026, satellite applications by 4 Dec 2026, terrestrial build-out milestones in 2029 and 2034.
- Scotiabank cut price targets only 2-4% and left 2026-28 estimates unchanged while the stocks fell 10-13%. One analyst, not consensus.
- In July Verizon raised 2026 free-cash-flow guidance to $21.94-22.14bn and T-Mobile raised adjusted FCF to $18.4-18.8bn. AT&T reiterated $18bn+ and plans about $10bn of buybacks, roughly 6.6% of its post-drop market value.

**Best bear case.** The stocks are being re-rated on long-run value, and good quarters don't reverse that. More SpaceX milestones land inside the window. The 10-year is at 5.24%, so Verizon's 6.8% dividend yield is only about 1.6 points over Treasuries, with $128.7bn of net unsecured debt. AT&T's net debt was $126.4bn before it paid about $23bn in cash for EchoStar spectrum. Verizon guides wireless service revenue roughly flat, and T-Mobile's FCF raise came mainly from lower cash taxes.

**Valuation.**

| | VZ | T | TMUS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close, 9 Oct | $41.65 | $22.18 | $148.58 |
| P/E on 2026 guidance | 8.3x | 9.4-9.9x | 11.8x forward (aggregator) |
| FCF yield on guidance | about 12.7% | 11.8%+ | about 11.7% |
| Dividend yield | 6.8% | 5.0% | 3.1% |

At a 9-10% required return (my assumption), a 12% FCF yield implies free cash flow shrinking 2-4% a year forever. Guidance is for growth. That may turn out right, but it is already priced.

**Catalysts.** AT&T Q3 on 21 Oct (pre-market), Verizon on 26 Oct, T-Mobile on 28 Oct (after the close; it has guided a soft Q3, about 250k account adds), FOMC on 28 Oct, Grain's FCC notice on 5 Nov.

**What would prove me wrong.**

1. Any of the three cuts 2026 FCF guidance at Q3, or Verizon or AT&T post negative postpaid phone net adds.
2. SpaceX publishes Starlink Mobile pricing or a launch date before end-2027.
3. After all three have reported, VZ closes below $37.50 and T below $20.00 on or after 6 Nov.

**Weighting.** T-Mobile is only 20% because it has the highest multiple, the thinnest dividend, a Q3 it has guided soft, and it sold its 800 MHz licences to Grain for $2.9bn in cash plus Grain's 600 MHz licences; SpaceX has now agreed to buy that band for a reported $8bn (not a like-for-like comparison).
