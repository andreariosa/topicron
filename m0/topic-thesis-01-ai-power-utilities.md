# AI Power Demand Reshapes Regulated Utilities

**Why this is trending:** Hyperscaler AI infrastructure spending — running near $700–750B in 2026 across Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, and Alphabet — is driving a step-change in electricity demand from data centers. Regulated utilities with disclosed hyperscaler power-purchase exposure are being repriced by the market from defensive dividend names toward structural growth stories, while rising consumer electricity bills are becoming a politically contentious side effect.

**Linked companies:** Constellation Energy (CEG), NextEra Energy (NEE), Entergy (ETR), Edison International (EIX)

## Bull case

Hyperscaler AI capital spending could continue climbing through 2026, and data-center electricity load is structurally different from residential demand: it runs continuously, which may let utilities plan capacity additions with more confidence than against typical seasonal demand. Constellation Energy and Entergy are both named across independent sources as having direct exposure to hyperscaler power-purchase agreements, which could support above-trend earnings growth if utilities can convert that demand into approved rate base.

## Bear case

The same capex cycle that could lift utility earnings is also drawing political scrutiny: rising consumer electricity bills are becoming an election-cycle issue, and public figures have called for hyperscalers to bear more of the infrastructure cost directly rather than through general rate increases — a shift that, if regulators act on it, could cap the very upside utilities are currently being repriced for. Several of these names have already re-rated significantly over the past year, which may mean much of the optimism is already reflected in current prices.

## Risks

- **Regulatory/political:** proposals to shift AI-related infrastructure costs onto hyperscalers rather than ratepayers could reduce or delay the earnings uplift utilities are priced for.
- **Demand concentration:** the thesis leans heavily on continued hyperscaler capex; a slowdown from a handful of large tech companies would remove the core driver.
- **Valuation:** several names have already had large 12-month moves, limiting room for further re-rating.
- **Grid/interconnection:** physical grid capacity and interconnection queues could bottleneck how fast demand converts into billed revenue.
