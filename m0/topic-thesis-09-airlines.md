# Airlines: United's Execution Hasn't Caught Up to Its Valuation

*As of July 30, 2026. This is the second version of this write-up, rebuilt under ADR-0003's template with Q2 2026 earnings (reported since the first draft) and current valuation/catalyst data.*

## The hypothesis

Over the next 90 days, United will outperform Delta and American as its stronger Q2 execution — raised full-year guidance despite absorbing the largest fuel-cost increase of the three — gets recognized in a valuation that hasn't caught up to its results, while Delta's premium multiple already prices in its quality and American's weak balance sheet leaves it most exposed to any renewed oil spike.

## Why now

Q2 2026 earnings are now out for all three carriers, and they complicate the "Delta good, everyone else struggling" story this write-up originally told. United raised its full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $9.00-11.00 despite a $2.3 billion year-over-year fuel cost increase, recouping roughly half of it through pricing and capacity discipline — a stronger guidance trajectory than Delta's maintained $6.50-7.50 outlook. Morgan Stanley responded by raising its United price target to $190, implying roughly 60% upside from the last close. Delta posted record Q2 revenue but absorbed the highest quarterly fuel expense in its history ($3.93/gallon) and gave Q3 EPS guidance of $2.00-2.50. American remains the outlier: negative shareholders' equity of $4.1 billion and $34.7 billion in total debt leave it recouping only about half its own fuel-cost increase with far less cushion, and its stock fell 20% in July alone — the sector's worst monthly performance — before rebounding on oil-price de-escalation news out of Iran. WTI crude has ranged from $55 to a $114 peak over the past year and swung from roughly $101 to $85 a barrel within the past two weeks alone.

## What the market may be missing

United's actual operational print — the largest fuel-cost hit of the three, absorbed while still raising full-year guidance — reads as stronger than Delta's "maintained outlook," yet Delta still carries the more obvious premium framing (16x forward earnings against an 11x sector average) while United's own multiple isn't described anywhere in current coverage as commanding a similar premium. The market may be re-rating Delta for a quality story it already knows, while under-rating United for a quarter it just delivered.

## Bull case

United converted a genuinely worse fuel-cost shock than any peer into a raised full-year guide, with Q2 revenue up 16%, capacity up 3.5%, yield up 12%, and contracted business revenue up 27% — a demonstration that its international and corporate-travel exposure, previously framed as this thesis's biggest risk, is instead driving the strongest results in the group. Delta's loyalty and premium-cabin moat remains real and durable, supporting its own premium multiple even if the incremental upside from here is more limited. Both benefit if the current oil-price retreat (from ~$101 to ~$85 on Iran-related de-escalation) holds or extends, easing the fuel assumptions both built their guidance around.

## Best bear case

Airline earnings guidance is a moving target on a volatile input: Delta itself flagged that its own fuel-price assumptions could be revised again, and "rapid shifts may render an airline forecast outdated in just a few days," per the same coverage reporting United's raise. If oil resumes climbing toward its 12-month high rather than continuing to ease, all three carriers' guidance — United's most of all, since it is the newest and least tested — could prove to have been given at the top of a fuel-cost reprieve rather than a durable new baseline. American's balance sheet gives it the least room to absorb a second shock, and a reversal in the oil trend could reopen the exact gap this thesis is betting narrows.

## Valuation and expectations

Delta trades at roughly 16x forward earnings against an ~11x industry average — a premium the market has been willing to pay for its operational consistency and loyalty revenue, and current coverage explicitly notes "much of the recent optimism may already be reflected in the share price." United and American both screen as low-P/E, "value territory" names on 2026 projections, though neither carries as explicit a multiple in current coverage as Delta's — the thesis leans on United's guidance trajectory outrunning its price re-rating so far, not on a precisely quantified discount.

## Catalysts

Oil's near-term path is the most immediate, live catalyst: a sustained move back above $95-100 a barrel would pressure all three, while continued de-escalation could extend the recent relief rally. Beyond that, each carrier's next quarterly report (Q3, within the 90-day window) tests whether United's guidance raise holds up and whether Delta's premium multiple continues to expand on no new information. A wildcard worth naming rather than betting on: The Wall Street Journal reported United previously approached Delta about a merger (talks did not progress) and separately approached American (rejected) — consolidation speculation could reprice any of these names on rumor alone, independent of the fundamentals above.

## Invalidation conditions

Wrong if oil sustains above roughly $95-100 a barrel and United's next guidance update walks back the recent raise. Wrong if American's balance-sheet stress shows signs of becoming a going-concern-adjacent issue rather than a margin issue, which would argue for avoiding the name entirely rather than underweighting it. Wrong if Delta's multiple continues expanding despite no incremental news, indicating the market assigns more durable value to Delta's quality than this thesis assumes.

## Risks

- **Oil-price reversal risk:** every element of this thesis — United's guidance, Delta's fuel assumption, American's cushion — is built on recent, volatile crude levels that have already swung roughly $30/barrel in two weeks.
- **Balance-sheet risk (American specifically):** negative shareholders' equity and high leverage mean American has the least room for a second fuel shock or a demand slowdown.
- **M&A/rumor risk:** reported past merger approaches (United-Delta, United-American) mean speculative M&A headlines could move any of these names independent of the operating thesis.
- **Concentration risk:** overweighting United concentrates the basket's outcome on one carrier's guidance holding up through Q3.

## Basket and benchmark

**Basket** (differentiated by conviction, not equal-weight — the thesis argues United's execution is under-recognized while American carries the most balance-sheet risk, so the basket reflects that): United (UAL) 45%, Delta (DAL) 35%, American (AAL) 20%.

**Benchmark:** JETS (U.S. Global Jets ETF), which holds all major US carriers and isolates the differentiated-weighting claim from a simple "airlines will do well" sector bet.

**Expected outcome:** Basket outperforms JETS by at least 4 percentage points over 90 days.

**Reason for benchmark:** JETS captures sector-wide moves driven by oil and demand that all carriers share; outperforming it specifically tests whether this thesis's relative call — United ahead of Delta ahead of American — was right, not whether airlines broadly recovered.
