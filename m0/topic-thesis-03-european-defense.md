# European Defense Rearmament: From Momentum Trade to Selective Story

**Why this is trending:** After a roughly 65% gain for the sector in 2025, 2026 has brought a real correction — the Stoxx Europe Aerospace & Defence index is down slightly year-to-date against a positive broader market, and Rheinmetall alone has fallen roughly 50% from its all-time high. The debate now is whether this is a healthy entry point into a multi-decade structural spending cycle, or a sign that easy sector-wide gains are over and stock-picking now matters more than broad exposure.

**Linked companies:** Rheinmetall (RHM, XETRA/Frankfurt), BAE Systems (BA., LSE), Thales (HO, Euronext Paris), Leonardo (LDO, Borsa Italiana)

## Bull case

Europe's rearmament looks less like a geopolitical news cycle and more like a durable, multi-year budget commitment: NATO members have agreed to raise core defense spending toward 3.5% of GDP by 2035, with an additional 1.5% earmarked for broader security investment. Germany's constitutional "debt-brake" reform removed a key fiscal constraint, pushing its 2026 defense budget up roughly 25% year-on-year, with several frontline and Baltic states already spending well above 3% of GDP. That kind of multi-year, government-backed order visibility is unusual for a historically cyclical industrial sector.

## Bear case

The same structural story that drove 2025's rally is now facing a "wall of worry": elevated valuations built up over three years, the possibility that a Ukraine peace settlement could reduce the political urgency behind current spending plans, and reported U.S. diplomatic pressure for European governments to buy American-made systems instead of domestic ones. Markets appear to be shifting from rewarding sector-wide enthusiasm toward scrutinizing individual companies' order books and cash flows — which is a higher bar than the broad re-rating investors have benefited from so far.

## Risks

- **Political/peace risk:** any de-escalation or settlement in Ukraine could reduce the political urgency behind spending commitments this thesis assumes are durable.
- **Fiscal/competing-priorities risk:** sustained elevated defense spending consumes fiscal space that other government priorities may compete for over a multi-year horizon.
- **"Buy American" pressure:** reported U.S. pressure for European governments to purchase American systems could redirect procurement away from these specific companies even if total European defense spending keeps rising.
- **Valuation/re-rating risk:** after a large multi-year rally followed by a 2026 pullback, further gains likely require the procurement cycle to convert into actual deliveries and cash flow, not just budget headlines.
