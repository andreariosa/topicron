# Risks and validation strategy

*This document implements the risk-related decisions in ADR-0001 — D-01, D-02, D-06, D-09, D-11, D-12, D-15, D-17 — as targeted additions to the original risk analysis, not a rewrite of it, plus the operational risks named in ADR-0002. The regulatory analysis in particular is substantively unchanged; ADR-0001 confirms it "remains accurate," and ADR-0002 doesn't touch it further. Decisions are cited inline as "ADR-0001, D-XX" or "ADR-0002, D-XX."*

## Technical risks

- **Reddit API economics and access model.** Dormant for the MVP — there's no Reddit connector to hit rate limits on (ADR-0001, D-10; `docs/MVP_SCOPE.md`). It's still the reason the connector is deferred rather than built: the free tier is capped (roughly 10,000 requests/month, 100/minute) and restricted to non-commercial use, and the commercial tier starts in the five-figure-per-year range with no middle option. **Updated 2026-07-31, two findings that raise the bar further:** (1) Reddit replaced self-service app registration with a manual-approval ticket process (the Responsible Builder Policy, since late 2025) — multi-week waits, frequent non-response or generic rejection, even for the free/research tier. (2) The policy explicitly prohibits using Reddit data to train ML/AI models without separate approval — if this connector is ever built to feed content into the Claude-based synthesis pipeline (rather than for human-read research only, which is what any near-term application actually describes), that specific use needs its own disclosure to Reddit, not an extension of a narrower approval. Whenever the connector is reinstated, design it to work within the free tier for as long as possible, and treat the paid tier as a funded decision.
- **Synchronous LLM dependency.** Never call the LLM inside a user-facing request path — pre-compute everything via background jobs (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`). A provider outage, rate limit, or — during free-tier-first — a founder simply not having run the latest session yet should degrade "how fresh is the newest topic," never "does the app load."
- **Entity resolution.** Ticker extraction from social/news text collides with common words ("ALL", "ARE", "IT", "ON", "SO" are all real tickers) — a recurring, underestimated source of bad data, not an edge case. During the free-tier-first MVP, the LLM fallback that would normally resolve these ambiguous cases is deferred (ADR-0002, D-20) — they're logged for manual review instead, which keeps the gap visible but does mean rule-based matching alone is carrying more of this risk than originally designed, for now.
- **Market calendar handling.** Weekends, holidays, and pre/post-market timing are a frequent source of subtle bugs in daily price-snapshot and performance-calculation logic — the 90-day holding rule's exit-date computation (`docs/DATA_MODEL.md`, The 90-day holding rule) is a concrete, load-bearing example of exactly this risk, not a hypothetical one.
- **Model drift.** A model or prompt upgrade can shift thesis style or quality without warning — version every generation (`docs/AI_SYSTEM.md`) so regressions are traceable. This applies equally whether a generation came from a scheduled API call or a founder-run Claude Code session — the discipline of logging `model_used` and `prompt_version` doesn't relax just because a human is in the loop.
- **Topic fragmentation.** The topic synthesizer runs every six hours over a fresh batch of content with no guaranteed continuity unless it's explicitly built in — without it, "AI datacenter power demand" detected at T and "Utilities strained by AI buildout" detected at T+3h become two unrelated topics instead of one continuing narrative (ADR-0001, D-12). This is the highest-uncertainty component of the entire pipeline: a fragmented topic can't carry a coherent thesis, portfolio, or track record. `docs/AI_SYSTEM.md`'s Topic continuity mechanism is the mitigation; `pgvector`-based embedding similarity is the fallback if prompt-level resolution doesn't hold up against real data. (During free-tier-first, "every six hours" describes the reinstated cadence — see `docs/AI_SYSTEM.md`'s free-tier-first execution mode for the founder-run default.)
- **Free-tier infrastructure dormancy.** Running on free tiers (ADR-0002) means two specific, well-documented behaviors become real operating conditions rather than hypotheticals: GitHub Actions disables scheduled workflows in a public repository after 60 days with no commits (not no job runs), and Supabase pauses a free project after 7 days with no database activity. Neither is a defect — both are normal free-tier behavior, and both have near-zero-effort mitigations (a monthly keepalive commit; a one-click dashboard resume) — but a founder who steps away for a couple of months should expect to find the pipeline paused, not assume something broke (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, Free-tier operating notes).

## Product risks

- **Cold-start of the differentiator itself.** The product's core claim — a public, unedited track record — takes six to twelve months of elapsed time to mean anything, and says nothing at all on launch day. This is distinct from the feed-emptiness cold start below: even a perfectly populated topic feed on day one still has zero performance history behind it, because that history can only accumulate through real elapsed calendar time, not through more engineering effort (ADR-0001, D-01). The plan's answer is an explicit interim value proposition — the thesis itself is worth reading before any performance number exists to validate it (`docs/VISION.md`, Why this is useful before the track record exists) — but that answer has to actually hold up, or the first six months are unfunded by any real value proposition.
- **Generic output ("AI slop").** The single biggest execution risk day-to-day. A thesis that reads as "this could go up if things go well" provides no value over a headline. Prompt quality and grounding matter more than any other engineering decision in this plan.
- **Empty feed on day one.** An empty topic feed feels broken, distinct from the differentiator cold-start above — this is about the UI looking populated, not about the track record having history. Consider backfilling some historical ingestion before the first beta users arrive so the app doesn't feel dead on day one.
- **Retention.** Research and reference tools notoriously have low daily engagement without a habit trigger (a digest, a followed topic updating, a notification). Don't assume "interesting once" implies "used weekly."
- **Commoditized entry point.** Multiple existing free tools already track Reddit mentions and sentiment (mention counts, bullish/bearish scores). That is not this product's differentiator and shouldn't receive disproportionate engineering investment — the thesis-generation and accountability loop is the actual bet. The MVP resolves this as cleanly as possible: there is no sentiment-scoring subsystem at all, for any source (ADR-0001, D-06; `docs/AI_SYSTEM.md`, Momentum). Momentum is a simple mention-count query, not a modeled signal — the engineering investment goes where the differentiator actually is.
- **Survivorship bias.** If losing theses are quietly stopped or de-emphasized while winners get highlighted, the "public, unedited track record" claim becomes false, and with it, the core differentiator.
- **No distribution plan.** The single largest omission in the original planning set: six documents, and none of them said how anyone would ever find this product (ADR-0001, D-11). Distribution isn't a marketing afterthought for a publishing product — it's the delivery mechanism. `docs/ROADMAP.md`'s distribution workstream (named communities, an owned email list, a fixed cadence) is the resolution, started in M0 rather than improvised at launch.
- **Persona drift.** "Target users" recurred throughout the original plan without ever being defined, and a retail swing trader, a professional analyst, and a curious generalist want three incompatible products (ADR-0001, D-17). The primary persona is now written down (`docs/VISION.md`, Who this is for), but the risk doesn't end at definition — it's in the slow, well-intentioned widening that happens under growth pressure: a feature added to serve a slightly different reader, a tone shift to appeal to a slightly bigger audience. Each individual widening can look reasonable; the cumulative effect dilutes the writing until it serves no one well. Every product decision should be checked against the primary persona and the anti-persona, not against whichever reader happens to be loudest.
- **Founder-availability bottleneck for AI generation.** Running `synth/` and `thesis/` as founder-triggered sessions rather than scheduled jobs (ADR-0002, D-20) makes the founder a single point of failure for how often new topics and theses appear, in a way an unattended pipeline never was. A busy month means fewer new theses, not silently continued output — which is the correct failure mode (visible thinness, not invisible degradation) but is still a real constraint on publishing cadence that the original always-on design didn't have. Worth tracking actual weekly time spent against ADR-0002's own reinstatement trigger (~3–4 hours/week) rather than assuming it'll stay comfortable indefinitely.

## Data quality risks

- **Manipulable sentiment.** Dormant for the MVP specifically, since there is no sentiment-scoring subsystem and no Reddit ingestion (`docs/AI_SYSTEM.md`, Momentum; ADR-0001, D-06, D-10) — but not irrelevant going forward. If Reddit and any sentiment-adjacent signal return post-M0 validation, coordinated posting and bot activity that skew mention volume, particularly for lower-liquidity tickers, becomes a real concern again. Simple heuristics (flagging spikes driven by a small number of accounts, or by suspiciously new accounts) are worth designing before that reinstatement, not after.
- **Free-tier data delays.** Free-tier market data can run 15+ minutes delayed. Fine for daily snapshots; never present it as real-time.
- **Source quality variance.** RSS feed quality varies widely — some sources will need to be dropped or down-weighted after the first few weeks of real ingestion. This matters more now than it would have originally, since curated RSS is the only ingestion source in the MVP (`docs/MVP_SCOPE.md`).
- **Content licensing.** RSS feeds grant syndication rights to headlines, summaries, and links — not a license to reproduce full article bodies (ADR-0001, D-15). `docs/AI_SYSTEM.md`'s citation contract enforces this at the generation layer (a paraphrase plus a source id, never a quoted excerpt), but it's worth stating as a standalone risk: the failure mode isn't a model mistake, it's a design decision that has to hold even under pressure to make a thesis's evidence feel more concrete by quoting it directly.

## Regulatory considerations

*This is not legal advice — consult a securities attorney before launch, and definitely before monetizing or adding any personalization.*

The relevant U.S. legal framework is the Investment Advisers Act of 1940. Under the "publisher's exclusion" established in *Lowe v. SEC* (1985), a publisher of investment commentary is excluded from "investment adviser" registration requirements as long as its content: (1) is impersonal — not tailored to any specific reader's portfolio or situation; (2) is bona fide — genuine, disinterested commentary, not a vehicle for touting securities the publisher is paid to promote; and (3) is of general and regular circulation, rather than issued sporadically in reaction to specific market events. A 2024 case involving Seeking Alpha reaffirmed this exclusion for a modern web publishing platform, a directly relevant precedent for a product like this one.

The MVP's publication-first design (`docs/VISION.md`, Why publication, before dashboard; ADR-0001, D-18) sits comfortably inside this third criterion specifically because it commits to a fixed weekly cadence rather than an on-demand dashboard a reader visits whenever the market moves — regular circulation was always the easier criterion to satisfy with a scheduled publication than with an interactive app.

This has concrete design implications, not just legal ones:

- Keep `suggested_basket` a naive, non-personalized allocation applied uniformly to everyone reading a given thesis (`docs/AI_SYSTEM.md`) — personalized recommendations tailored to an individual's holdings, goals, or risk tolerance are exactly what risks crossing from "publisher" into "adviser."
- Publish on a regular schedule and cadence, not only in reaction to hot market moments — part of what protects the exclusion is that the commentary isn't timed to episodic market activity. This holds regardless of whether a given issue's theses came from a scheduled job or a founder-run session (ADR-0002) — the cadence commitment is about publication, not generation mechanics.
- Keep marketing claims about AI capability conservative and substantiated. In March 2024 the SEC settled "AI washing" charges against two investment advisers for misrepresenting their AI capabilities, with combined penalties around $400,000. That case involved registered advisers, but the underlying lesson — don't claim AI capabilities the product doesn't actually have — applies to any company marketing an AI product in this space, regulated or not. A published backtest — a performance figure generated with a model that has already read how the underlying events turned out — counts as exactly this kind of unsubstantiated capability claim, which is why the MVP never publishes one (ADR-0001, D-02; `docs/AI_SYSTEM.md`, Evaluating AI-generated ideas).
- The regulatory environment here is still moving: the SEC proposed a rule in 2023 specifically addressing conflicts of interest in "predictive data analytics" (which would have covered AI/LLM-driven tools), then formally withdrew it in June 2025. The absence of an AI-specific rule right now doesn't mean the space is settled — revisit this section periodically.

## AI reliability issues

See `docs/AI_SYSTEM.md` for the full mitigation approach. In short: ground every generation in retrieved facts, validate structured output against schema, cite every claim back to its source, version every prompt and model, and evaluate theses against real subsequent market data — never AI judging AI.

## Validation strategy

Before committing months to the full build:

1. **Concierge test.** The fastest validation path leverages an existing manual workflow directly: hand-produce 10-20 topic-plus-thesis write-ups using real, current market narratives, and share them with real target users. If the write-ups aren't interesting to real people, no amount of engineering fixes that. Alongside the write-ups, keep an intervention log recording every topic rejected before selection, every fabricated or unsupported claim removed, and every sentence rewritten (ADR-0001, D-09). This is the mechanism that keeps the concierge test honest: without it, a founder cherry-picking interesting topics and lightly editing prose validates a quality bar the automated pipeline may never reach. The log is a required artifact of the M0 gate, not an optional nice-to-have (`docs/ROADMAP.md`, M0).
2. **Landing page and waitlist.** A simple page describing the core value proposition (topic discovery, AI thesis, tracked accountability), shared through the M0 distribution list (`docs/ROADMAP.md`, Distribution workstream), tests organic interest before any product code exists.
3. **Ship read-only before the dashboard.** Launch topic and thesis pages to a small beta and watch actual behavior — do people come back on day 2 or day 7, do they read multiple theses, do they share them — before investing in the engineering-heavier interactive dashboard. Performance *data capture* starts much earlier than this, in M1/M2, precisely because its value compounds with time (`docs/ROADMAP.md`; ADR-0001, D-03); what's still gated behind this validation step is performance *presentation* — the charts — and the dashboard entirely.
4. **Ask directly.** Use the standard "how would you feel if this product no longer existed" question with beta users; a low proportion of "very disappointed" answers is a real signal to revisit the concept before scaling further.

**North star metric.** The percentage of readers who return to read a *new* topic within 7 days of their first session distinguishes "interesting once" from "a habit" better than signups or raw page views — and it's the right metric to keep (ADR-0001, D-11).

It is not, however, a metric that means anything at small sample sizes. At n=20 — roughly the MVP beta's early scale — this number swings by 15 points on the behavior of two people; treating it as a pass/fail gate that early mistakes noise for signal. It's directional at beta scale, and becomes a real, gate-worthy threshold only once the denominator reaches something like n≈200 (`docs/ROADMAP.md`, M5). Until then, it's paired with — not replaced by — the qualitative "how would you feel" question above and unprompted sharing behavior, so a single noisy number is never the sole basis for a stop-or-continue decision this early.
