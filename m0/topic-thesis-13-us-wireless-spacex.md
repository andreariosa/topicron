# US Wireless Carriers: The SpaceX Selloff Prices a 2027 Threat Into 2026 Stocks

*As of the 9 October 2026 close; written 11 October 2026 and re-checked on the morning of 12 October, before the US open. Prices, multiples and ETF holdings come from stockanalysis.com [19], an aggregator. I cross-checked the 9 October closes of all three carriers on companiesmarketcap.com and those of Verizon and SPY on stockinvest.us, and they agree to the cent, but exchange data was not reachable from the research environment. SpaceX's own blog post and FCC filing could not be opened, so the deal terms come from Reuters [11], Light Reading [20] and other reports [12][13]. The $8 billion price is a Wall Street Journal report that neither SpaceX nor Grain has confirmed.*

## The hypothesis

Over the 90 days after publication, a basket of Verizon (40%), AT&T (40%) and T-Mobile (20%) will outperform the S&P 500 (SPY) by at least 5 percentage points on price return, as the 9 October selloff, which priced in a SpaceX mobile service that the company itself does not expect to launch until around the end of 2027, partly reverses once the three Q3 reports show no sign of damage.

## Why now

After the close on Thursday 8 October, SpaceX said it will buy Grain Management's nationwide 800 MHz low-band licences, up to 14 MHz of paired spectrum reaching nearly the whole US population, to give Starlink Mobile an indoor coverage layer [11][12]. The price was not disclosed; the Wall Street Journal reported $8 billion in cash [13]. The deal needs FCC approval [11]; SpaceX's statement to the FCC asks for it to be expedited and says SpaceX will also run standards-based wireless equipment on towers, rooftops and other structures, built to the construction benchmarks of the order that approved T-Mobile's sale to Grain [20]. Two days earlier the FCC had authorised a 15,000-satellite Starlink Mobile constellation [14].

On Friday 9 October, close to close (my arithmetic from [19]), T-Mobile fell 13.3% to $148.58, AT&T 10.8% to $22.18 and Verizon 10.1% to $41.65, on volume about 4.5 times the prior nine sessions' average, while SPY rose 0.6%. Verizon and AT&T also went ex-dividend that day, which accounts for about 1.5 and 1.1 points of their moves [19]. The group was already weak: over 52 weeks T-Mobile is down 34%, AT&T 15.5% and Verizon is roughly flat [19], and in the first week of July AT&T was heading for its worst week since March 2020 on the same satellite fears [17].

## What the market may be missing

The market turned a 2027 event into a 2026 price in one session. Three observations:

1. **The timeline is long and dated.** The FCC's Starlink Mobile authorisation ties SpaceX's authority over the EchoStar spectrum to a second closing step expected on or about 30 November 2027, and SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell expects service by the end of 2027 [14]. T-Mobile said satellite coverage was 0.0002% of its network usage in May [17]. The Grain licences carry their own FCC dates: partner selection reported to the FCC by 5 November 2026, satellite applications by 4 December 2026, terrestrial build-out milestones in July 2029 and July 2034 [1]. The next 90 days hold process headlines, not subscriber data.
2. **Targets and estimates barely moved.** Scotiabank cut price targets by 2-4% and left its 2026-2028 estimates unchanged, citing limited visibility into SpaceX's scale, while the stocks fell 10-13% [15]. One analyst is not consensus; it is the only published revision I found.
3. **The companies' own numbers moved the other way.** Verizon raised its 2026 free-cash-flow guidance and its buyback target in July [2], T-Mobile raised its adjusted free-cash-flow guidance [4], and AT&T reiterated guidance and plans about $10 billion of buybacks in 2026 [3], roughly 6.6% of its post-drop market value (my arithmetic, share count from [19]).

## Bull case

The mechanism is in the calendar. AT&T reports Q3 on Wednesday 21 October before the open [5], Verizon on Monday 26 October [6] and T-Mobile on Wednesday 28 October after the close [7]. These are the first hard numbers since the shock, and Q2 left little sign of damage: Verizon reported its best consumer second-quarter postpaid phone additions in five years, Q2 free cash flow up 24.4% and adjusted EPS up 6.6% [2]; AT&T added 432,000 postpaid phones and 367,000 fiber customers with adjusted EPS of $0.65 against $0.54 [3]; T-Mobile added 277,000 postpaid accounts [4]. There is a weak precedent for the reaction function: AT&T made a 52-week closing low in early July [17] and was at $25.45 by 24 September [19]. That is one observation and the causal link to earnings is my inference. Finally, buybacks retire more shares per dollar at lower prices, a mechanical support management can lean into at Q3.

## Best bear case

The strongest bear case is not that SpaceX takes customers within 90 days. It is that the stocks are being re-rated on long-run value and good quarters do not reverse that. One announcement moved AT&T 10.8% in a session after two weeks in which no daily move exceeded 2% [19], which shows how headline-driven the group is, and further SpaceX milestones fall inside the window: Grain's 5 November notice to the FCC [1], FCC action on the licence assignment (undated), and any distribution or pricing news. SpaceX's own filing also describes a terrestrial network on towers and rooftops [20], so the competitive path is not only the satellite timeline in the hypothesis; the FCC dates the terrestrial build-out milestones for these licences to July 2029 and July 2034 [1]. In July, analysts at TD Cowen and BNP Paribas said SpaceX's entry could be highly bearish for wireless and that the overhang may last into next year [17].

Macro adds to it. On 9 October the 10-year Treasury yielded 5.24% and the 2-year 4.80% [8], so Verizon's 6.8% dividend yield sits only about 1.6 points above the risk-free rate, while Verizon carries $128.7 billion of net unsecured debt [2] and AT&T carried $126.4 billion of net debt at 30 June [3], before its roughly $23 billion EchoStar purchase closed on 28 July [16]. The earnings base is also less solid than the headline: Verizon guides wireless service revenue roughly flat for 2026 [2], and T-Mobile attributed its free-cash-flow raise mainly to lower cash taxes [18]. Good prints may be what the market already expects.

That claim can be true, but it is already priced, and a true-but-priced threat is a different claim from an unpriced one. Part of the 2026 cash flow is helped by lower cash taxes [18], so the real yield is somewhat lower.

## Catalysts

All dated, inside the 90-day window:

- **Wed 21 Oct:** AT&T Q3, before the open [5].
- **Mon 26 Oct:** Verizon Q3, release 7:00 a.m. ET [6].
- **Tue-Wed 27-28 Oct:** FOMC decision on the 28th [9], the same day as T-Mobile's Q3 release after the close [7]. T-Mobile guided Q3 to about 250,000 account additions with elevated churn [18], so its print has a known soft spot.
- **Thu 5 Nov:** Grain must finish its satellite-partner selection and notify the FCC [1].
- **Fri 4 Dec:** Grain's satellite applications are due if a partner is selected [1].
- **Undated:** FCC action on the Grain-to-SpaceX licence assignment; a definitive agreement for the carriers' satellite joint venture, announced as an agreement in principle on 14 May [10].

## Invalidation conditions

Wrong if any of these is observed within the window:

1. **Fundamentals.** Any of the three lowers its 2026 free-cash-flow guidance at its Q3 report (Verizon $21.94-22.14 billion, AT&T $18 billion or more, T-Mobile $18.4-18.8 billion adjusted), or Verizon or AT&T reports negative postpaid phone net additions (Q2: +184,000 and +432,000 [2][3]).
2. **Timeline.** SpaceX publishes consumer pricing for Starlink Mobile, or announces a commercial launch date earlier than the end of 2027. Either pulls the threat forward, which is the premise this thesis rests on.
3. **Price despite good news.** Once all three have reported, a close on or after 6 November below $37.50 for Verizon and $20.00 for AT&T (about 10% under the 9 October closes). That would mean the market is selling regardless of fundamentals, which is the bear case, not this one.

## Risks

- **Concentration.** Three highly correlated stocks, one theme, one competitor, one rate environment. This is one bet, not three.
- **Rates and the Fed.** A hawkish surprise on 28 October would hit high-yield, high-debt names whatever the quarter shows.
- **Price return versus dividends.** The product tracks closing prices (`docs/DATA_MODEL.md`). T-Mobile goes ex-dividend on about 25 November [19]; the January ex-dates of Verizon and AT&T (9 October this time) fall close to day 90, and whether they land inside the window depends on dates not yet announced. If all three go ex inside the window, at today's yields this costs the basket roughly 1 point of price return against SPY (my estimate). The 5-point threshold is not adjusted for it.
- **Relative-return trap.** In a strong market SPY can outrun a defensive basket even if the thesis is right on fundamentals.
- **Data quality.** Closes come from aggregators (three agree, none is the exchange); SpaceX's blog post and FCC filing were not read directly; the $8 billion price is one report.
- **Unscheduled news.** Cable or handset partnerships with SpaceX, which analysts have speculated about [17], or a definitive carrier joint venture [10], could move all three on rumor alone.

## Basket and benchmark

**Basket**, differentiated by conviction under D-32: Verizon (VZ) 40%, AT&T (T) 40%, T-Mobile (TMUS) 20%.

- **Verizon 40%.** Cheapest on guidance (8.3x EPS, about 12.7% free-cash-flow yield), raised both free-cash-flow guidance and the buyback, 2.5x leverage [2].
- **AT&T 40%.** Guidance reiterated and a buyback plan worth about 6.6% of market value; fiber additions (367,000) add a source of growth that does not depend on mobile [3]. Not higher because the roughly $23 billion cash purchase of EchoStar spectrum closed after the last reported leverage figure [16].
- **T-Mobile 20%.** It fell most, so it has the most to recover if the thesis is right, but conviction is lowest: the highest multiple (11.8x against 8.3x and 9.4-9.9x), the thinnest dividend (3.1%), a Q3 it has itself guided soft [18], and it sold its 800 MHz licences to Grain for $2.9 billion in cash plus Grain's 600 MHz licences [1], the band SpaceX has now agreed to buy at a reported $8 billion [13] (not a like-for-like comparison, because T-Mobile also received the 600 MHz licences).

**Benchmark:** SPY (S&P 500).

**Expected outcome:** the basket outperforms SPY by at least 5 percentage points over 90 days, on price return.

**Reason for benchmark:** the claim is that this sector's shock overshot relative to the market. SPY removes market-wide movement and leaves the sector-specific move. Five points is roughly half of the 9 October move; it is stated in advance and is arbitrary by design (D-27).

**Secondary comparison:** an equal-weight VZ, T and TMUS basket, which isolates the weighting choice (Verizon and AT&T over T-Mobile). No clean sector ETF exists for US wireless: IYZ, the usual choice, held Cisco 22.8%, Arista 11.8%, Verizon 10.4%, AT&T 4.3%, SpaceX 3.9% and T-Mobile 3.3% on 2 October [19], so it mostly measures networking equipment. `docs/DATA_MODEL.md` expects `sector_benchmark_ticker` to be an ETF; this thesis would need a synthetic basket there.

**Tracking window:** entry at the first close after publication, exit 90 calendar days later (D-13). If published on Monday 12 October, entry is the 12 October close and day 90 is Sunday 10 January 2027, so exit is the 11 January close.
